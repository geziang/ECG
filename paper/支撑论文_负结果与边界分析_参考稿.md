## Abstract

Companion studies of self-supervised learning (SSL) for multi-lead electrocardiograms typically report the modification that improved a baseline, leaving unreported the far larger set of modifications that did not. This paper documents the negative-results program executed around the lead-aware redundancy-reduction baseline (LRRE, instantiated as B0) that underlies our companion paper on Temporal Response Calibration (TRC). Under a preregistered discipline—a seed-0 coarse probe gated by a three-seed sign-consistency rule, validation-only model selection, and per-family stop criteria—we evaluated more than 40 incremental modifications across two hosts: loss-weight and objective rewrites (including whitening/VICReg-style formulations, anatomy- and region-aware lead-relationship objectives, patient-positive and patient-decorrelation terms), architectural add-ons (lead attention and channel attention, Mixer- and JEPA-style blocks, automated module composition, cross-lead teacher–student distillation, physiological–spatial priors, lead-group alignment, Sinc convolutional front-ends, MixUp, and an HRV auxiliary head), reconstruction auxiliaries (single- and multi-segment masking and cardiac-cycle-constrained masked reconstruction), augmentation-strength and capacity sweeps (crop ratio, temporal masking, loss weights, projector width, pooling exponent), corpus mixing without domain adaptation, and a reduced-cost training shortcut. Every family closed negative or indistinguishable from the anchor: rewrites of the inter-lead objective cost between 0.3 and 15.0 AUPRC points; distillation degraded both clean performance (−2.3 points) and double-lead-dropout robustness (−0.9 points); mixing five pretraining corpora without domain adaptation cost 3.07 points; and the hyperparameter neighborhood of the anchor formed a plateau in which no single-axis deviation was stable. Negative controls and a rejected false positive (a probe at +0.27 points on seed 0 that reversed to −0.77/−0.54 on seeds 2/4) demonstrate that the harness detects broken configurations and that single-run gains of 0.2–0.4 points are indistinguishable from seed noise against a baseline whose cross-seed spread is roughly 0.8 points. We synthesize five root causes for these failures—protocol non-equivalence, objective mismatch with the redundancy-reduction geometry, the noise floor of a strong baseline, selection-induced test reuse, and uncontrolled data lineage—and state the reusable conclusions. This boundary map motivates the minimal-module design of the companion paper, in which the only modification to pass all preregistered gates was TRC, a 1,024-parameter recalibration module.

**Keywords:** electrocardiogram; self-supervised learning; negative results; Barlow Twins; lead-fusion; ablation; reproducibility; evaluation protocol

## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning literature is skewed toward positive results: modifications that help are published, while modifications that do not are discarded, so the field re-tests them independently at real cost. This problem is documented in metric learning and recommender systems, where re-evaluations under matched protocols eliminated much of the reported progress over simple baselines [16, 17], and in computer-vision classification, where baseline calibration changed the interpretation of widely used benchmarks [18]. Electrocardiogram (ECG) self-supervised learning (SSL) is exposed to the same risk for a structural reason: published gains typically come from a complete protocol—encoder, objective, augmentation, pretraining corpus, and downstream evaluation acting together—whereas follow-up work usually transplants one module into a different protocol and attributes the outcome to the module name [7, 9].

This paper is the companion of a positive result. In the companion manuscript, LRTC-Net augments a lead-aware redundancy-reduction encoder (LRRE) with Temporal Response Calibration (TRC)—a zero-initialized, channel-wise recalibration module with 1,024 parameters—and passes preregistered three-seed gates on the CPSC2018 third-domain task and on label efficiency. The present paper reports what the same program rejected: the systematic exploration of incremental modifications around the B0 anchor that preceded and surrounded TRC, conducted under an explicit stop discipline, and closed without a surviving benefit. Reporting this material serves three purposes. First, it defines the empirical boundary within which the TRC claim holds: TRC is not the best of a garden of working modules, but the sole survivor of a closed search space. Second, it quantifies the noise floor against which any single-run gain must be judged for this class of protocols. Third, it documents reusable negative knowledge—families of modifications that fail for identifiable mechanistic reasons, with the protocol conditions under which the closure applies.

The exploration was organized as a preregistered pipeline. A candidate modification was first run as a single-seed (seed 0) coarse probe; only probes whose paired delta exceeded a threshold advanced to a three-seed (0, 2, 4) sign-consistency gate; model selection during exploration used validation data only, with the test set evaluated once after freezing; and each modification family carried a stop rule prohibiting further sweeps of the same axis after closure. Two hosts (an RTX 4090 and an RTX 3080) contributed probes under same-machine, same-seed paired deltas against their local anchors.

The contributions of this paper are:

1. A complete, audited account of more than 40 closed probe configurations across two hosts, spanning objective rewrites, architectural add-ons, reconstruction auxiliaries, augmentation and capacity sweeps, corpus mixing, and protocol shortcuts, with all results retained in the project ledger.
2. A quantification of the exploration noise floor: against a strong anchor with roughly 0.8 points of cross-seed spread, single-run gains of 0.2–0.4 points are not actionable, demonstrated by a false positive that passed seed 0 and reversed under seeds 2 and 4.
3. Harness-validation evidence from negative controls: deliberately broken configurations produce large, immediate degradations, confirming that the pipeline detects faults rather than absorbing them.
4. A five-root-cause analysis of why module-level transplantation fails in this setting, and a set of reusable conclusions that constrain the design space for multi-lead ECG SSL.
5. The boundary that motivates the companion paper's minimal-module approach: within this closed space, only TRC passed all gates.

All closures are stated as protocol-scoped: they apply to the implementations, hyperparameter intervals, and evaluation protocols tested, and do not constitute falsification of the underlying method families.

## 2. Background and Relation to the Companion Paper

### 2.1. The Anchor Protocol

The base configuration is the lead-aware redundancy-reduction setting described in the companion paper: eight leads (II, III, V1–V6) encoded by independent five-block one-dimensional convolutional branches; a Barlow-Twins-style objective [6] applied within each lead across two augmented views and across ordered lead pairs [7]; PTB-XL folds 1–8 for in-domain pretraining [1]; fold 9 for validation; fold 10 as the locked test set; and a five-class diagnostic-superclass downstream task with macro-AUPRC as the primary metric. The anchor model B0 was trained for 200 epochs with batch size 128 under this protocol, with checkpoints, manifests, and hashes frozen in the project ledger. The companion paper reports final-protocol three-seed means; the present paper uses the exploration-phase anchors against which the archived deltas were paired (Section 3.1).

### 2.2. Why Negative Results Are Load-Bearing Here

Two facts about the anchor make systematic negative results informative rather than merely voluminous. First, B0 is strong: relative to a randomly initialized encoder, SSL pretraining raised linear-probe macro-AUPRC from 0.1930 to 0.7177 (Section 4.1), so the anchor already extracts most of what the unlabeled corpus offers, and the remaining headroom for small modules is narrow. Second, the anchor is noisy at the scale of typical module gains: its historical multi-seed linear-probe macro-AUPRC spread is approximately 0.8 points, which exceeds the single-run gains reported for many candidate modules. A search that does not control seed variance will therefore manufacture positive results at a predictable rate, which is precisely what the preregistered gates are designed to prevent.

### 2.3. Related Method Families Evaluated

The closed families intersect several published directions. Anatomy- and region-aware lead-relationship objectives adapt contrastive formulations that structure lead groups explicitly [9]; patient-level positives follow the patient/time hierarchy exploited by CLOCS [5]; whitening and variance–invariance–covariance objectives follow W-MSE and VICReg [10]; MixUp-based alignment follows interpolation-based regularization [11]; Sinc-parameterized front ends follow raw-waveform convolutions [12]; channel and lead attention follow SENet/ECA-style recalibration [14, 15]; and masked-reconstruction auxiliaries follow the masked-ECG-pretraining family surveyed in the companion paper. For each family, the closure statement is about our implementation under our protocol, not about the family's validity in its original setting.

## 3. Materials and Methods

### 3.1. Anchors, Pairing, and Protocol Scope

The exploration-phase anchor on host A (RTX 4090) is the B0 seed-0 linear probe with macro-AUROC 0.9126 and macro-AUPRC 0.7177 on the PTB-XL locked test set; host B (RTX 3080) maintained its own local anchor at 0.7158. All deltas in this paper are same-machine, same-seed, same-downstream-split paired differences against these anchors; absolute scores from the two hosts are never subtracted from one another. These anchors belong to the exploration protocol and predate the final unified three-seed protocol of the companion paper (which reports, for example, a three-seed B0 linear-probe macro-AUPRC of 0.7119); the two scales are therefore not directly comparable, and no attempt is made here to re-express archived deltas in the final protocol. The anchor's supplementary reference points are its fine-tuning results (FT-100 macro-AUROC/AUPRC 0.9197/0.7173; FT-10 0.8756/0.6433) and the random-initialization controls (linear probe 0.4700/0.1930; FT-10 0.8385/0.5836).

### 3.2. Preregistered Gates and Stop Rules

The exploration discipline had four components. (i) A coarse probe ran each candidate once at seed 0; a paired delta at or below zero, absent a mechanistic remediation hypothesis, closed the probe without additional seeds. (ii) A sign-consistency gate required candidates with a positive seed-0 delta to reproduce with consistent sign across seeds 0, 2, 4 before any claim was recorded; consistent sign was treated as a trend, not as statistical significance. (iii) Validation-only selection: model selection and go/no-go decisions during exploration used the validation split; the test split was evaluated once, after code, hyperparameters, and checkpoints were frozen. (iv) Family stop rules: once an axis (e.g., crop strength, projector width, pooling exponent) was closed, further single-point sweeps of that axis were prohibited absent new data or a new mechanistic hypothesis. An additional integrity rule voided any result set produced under the legacy downstream split after a patient-level leakage audit; the affected legacy material is reported here only as boundary indication, never as confirmatory evidence.

### 3.3. Negative Controls

Three configurations were deliberately broken to verify harness sensitivity: a D1L implementation with the target placed on the off-diagonal (`d1l_neg`), and two corrupted variants of the whitening probe (`h1_ln_neg8`, `h1_pos_neg8`). All three were expected to degrade sharply; Section 4.2 confirms they did.

### 3.4. Evidence Tiers

Results are reported under explicit tiers: (T1) archived matrix probes—seed-0 paired deltas, or three-seed sets where the gate was triggered, recorded in the closed-experiment ledger; (T2) historical families—single-seed explorations predating the final protocol, retained as boundary analysis; (T3) voided material—results from the legacy split, reported only as context. No T2 or T3 result is used to support any positive claim.

Two mechanistic readings recur across these families. First, objectives that hard-align representations across leads (LGA, patient decorrelation in its strong form, and the cross-lead components of E005) consistently damage transfer: the inter-lead term of the anchor objective is a redundancy-reduction term, and converting it into a similarity-maximization term erases the lead specificity that the independent-branch design exists to preserve. Second, adding optimization surface without adding inductive bias (E003 composition, larger or smaller projectors, alternative pooling) is absorbed by the strong baseline as variance, not converted into signal.

### 4.5. Region- and Anatomy-Aware Objectives: A Worthy Failure

The A-line results deserve individual comment because they target a plausible hypothesis: that explicit anatomical structure of the lead system should help a multi-lead encoder. The corrected anatomy-aware objective (`a1_d1l_fix`) cost 7.06 points; the region-aware intra-only variant cost 9.11 points; and adding the cross-region term recovered 7.30 points relative to intra-only, yet still closed 1.83 points below the anchor. The recovery shows that the cross-region positive design carries real mechanistic information—consistent with the published formulation [9]—while the aggregate outcome shows that adding these terms on top of the anchor objective under our batch size, projector, and in-domain pretraining corpus does not transfer. Because our implementation appends the region objective to the anchor loss rather than replacing it, and because the original method was validated under a different corpus and batch size, we state this strictly as a transfer boundary, not a falsification of anatomy-aware contrastive learning.

### 4.6. The Sole Survivor

Within the closed space summarized above, exactly one modification passed all preregistered gates: Temporal Response Calibration, a zero-initialized, 1,024-parameter channel-wise recalibration module derived from temporal L2 response statistics. Its confirmatory results—three-seed sign-consistent gains of +0.83 AUPRC points on the CPSC2018 third-domain task and +0.65 points on PTB-XL FT-10 under external pretraining, with preserved robustness—are reported in the companion paper, together with the settings in which it provides no resolvable gain (PTB-XL and Chapman cross-domain differences, and the CPSC2018 record-level comparison after partition correction). The CPSC2018 figure was +2.30 points on the original partition; a post hoc data-lineage audit found that two label-mapping revisions had been run over an uncleaned directory, inflating the partition, and the corrected re-evaluation reduced the gain to +0.83 points while preserving three-of-three seed consistency and gate passage. The contrast between the breadth of the closed space and the minimality of the surviving modification is the central empirical claim linking the two papers: near a strong lead-fusion baseline, the useful modification surface for this protocol is narrow, and what passes is small and mechanistically targeted rather than structural.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1. Five Root Causes

The closures admit a compact causal analysis, consistent across families.

1. **Protocol non-equivalence.** The anchor's published strength derives from the joint effect of independent lead encoders, the intra/inter objective, the pretraining corpus, and the downstream protocol. Transplanting a module name into a different protocol tests a different system. Our early single-module, single-seed explorations reproduced exactly this error before the paired-anchor discipline was installed.
2. **Objective–geometry mismatch.** The inter-lead term is a redundancy-reduction constraint. Rewriting it as similarity maximization (LGA, strong patient decorrelation, cross-lead raw-alignment distillation) removes lead-specific information by construction; the magnitude of the resulting degradations (−0.89 to −14.98 points) tracks how hard the rewrite pulls.
3. **The noise floor.** With roughly 0.8 points of anchor cross-seed spread, single-run gains of 0.2–0.4 points are uninformative. The `aug_crop_weak` episode (+0.27 at seed 0; −0.77/−0.54 at seeds 2/4) is the program's internal demonstration; the preregistered gate exists because such episodes are otherwise indistinguishable from discoveries.
4. **Selection-induced test reuse.** The legacy screening pipeline evaluated candidates on the test split. Because the A-line candidates were clearly negative, closure decisions were not distorted, but the practice was discontinued: validation-only selection, followed by a single frozen test evaluation, is a precondition for any future positive claim.
5. **Uncontrolled data lineage.** The multi-corpus closure (−3.07 points without domain adaptation) and the lineage analysis of the companion paper show that corpus identity moves results by magnitudes comparable to or larger than module effects. The companion paper's CPSC2018 partition audit is a direct internal instance: two label-mapping revisions run over an uncleaned directory inflated the apparent third-domain gain from +0.83 to +2.30 points until prediction-count reconciliation against the preparation manifest exposed it. Any cross-study attribution of performance differences to objective design is confounded until corpus, size, split integrity, and optimizer-update matching are reported.

### 5.2. Reusable Conclusions

1. A strong lead-fusion baseline leaves no room for random small-module scanning; exploration must be hypothesis-gated.
2. Inter-lead redundancy reduction must not be casually rewritten as similarity maximization; hard cross-lead alignment damages both transfer and, in the distillation case, lead-dropout robustness.
3. Single-seed gains of 0.2–0.4 points do not support claims in this protocol class; the three-seed sign-consistency gate is the minimum evidentiary standard, and it is a filter, not a significance test.
4. Data lineage, split hygiene, and downstream protocol dominate module identity as determinants of reported performance; a patient-level leakage audit voided one legacy result set entirely, and none of that material is used here as confirmatory evidence.
5. Preregistered stop rules convert failed searches from sunk cost into boundary knowledge: every family in Table 4 is closed with its protocol scope stated, which is what makes the survivor's evidentiary position meaningful.

### 5.3. Limitations

All closures are single-implementation, finite-hyperparameter-interval statements under an in-domain PTB-XL pretraining protocol with record-level splits; none falsifies a method family in its original setting. Most matrix probes are seed-0 paired deltas by design—the gate structure reserves multi-seed compute for positive trends—so the tables quantify screening outcomes, not confirmatory estimates. Historical families (Table 4) predate the final protocol and partially predate the split audit; they are reported as boundary indications only. The two-host structure doubles coverage but also means that several families were probed on only one host. Finally, this paper deliberately reports no new positive results; its claims are about the shape of the search space, and they inherit the scope of the protocols that produced them.

## 6. Conclusions

We reported the negative-results program surrounding the lead-aware redundancy-reduction baseline of our companion LRTC-Net paper: more than 40 closed probe configurations across two hosts, organized under preregistered seed-0/three-seed gates with family stop rules, validation-only selection, and negative-control harness validation. The outcome is a boundary map. The anchor's hyperparameter neighborhood is a plateau; every objective rewrite, architectural add-on, reconstruction auxiliary, corpus-mixing configuration, and protocol shortcut tested closed negative or indistinguishable, with degradations ranging from 0.1 to 15.0 AUPRC points; the harness demonstrably detects broken configurations; and the program's only false positive was caught by the three-seed gate. Against this map, the companion paper's surviving modification—TRC, 1,024 parameters, zero-initialized, mechanistically targeted—derives its evidentiary weight precisely from the space that was closed around it. We release this account with the expectation that its closures, their protocol scopes, and its reusable conclusions save future work from re-exploring dead axes, and with the reminder that in multi-lead ECG SSL, the protocol—not the module name—is the first-order variable.
